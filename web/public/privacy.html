Privacy policy

Last updated 11 November 2026. This text ships with the open source lazyfill software. Questions about the software: open an issue on the project’s GitHub page (please do not post personal data there). Questions about a particular copy: ask the person who runs it.

lazyfill is an independent project. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by or sponsored by any employer, job board, applicant tracking system, browser vendor or AI provider. Product and company names belong to their owners.

How lazyfill is run

lazyfill is software that you run yourself. There is no central lazyfill service and no shared server. The extension and the web tracker store your data in a Firebase project that was connected to your copy, usually by you. In this policy the operator means whoever owns that Firebase project. If you set it up yourself, the operator is you.

The people who wrote the lazyfill code do not receive your data, cannot see it, and run no analytics or tracking. Only the operator, Google (as the host of Firebase) and, if you use AI features, your AI provider are involved, as described below.

What lazyfill is

lazyfill is a Chrome extension (and a small web page) that fills in job applications from a profile you keep, tracks the applications you send, and can write draft answers with an AI service you connect. This policy says what it stores, where it goes, and how to delete it.

What lazyfill reads on web pages

lazyfill runs on the pages you visit so it can notice a job application form and fill it. It reads form fields, their labels, and (to name the job) the page title, company and description. This happens in your browser. Nothing is sent anywhere for pages that are not job applications. On a page it recognises as a job application, the job title, company, address and description are kept in your browser for the session and saved to your tracker when you autofill or submit.

AI features (optional)

If you add a Gemini or OpenAI API key, then only when you use an AI feature your browser sends the following straight to that provider (not to the operator and not to the lazyfill authors): for Import with AI, images of your resume pages, their text and their links; for Generate with lazyfill, the question you selected, a summary of your profile (your contact details are left out), and the job’s title, company and description. The provider handles it under its own terms and privacy policy. Free-tier plans of some providers, Google’s Gemini API among them, may use what you send to improve their products; paid plans generally do not. Please read your provider’s terms. If you never add a key, nothing is sent to any AI service.

Who can see your data

  • You. The Firestore rules that ship with lazyfill allow only your signed-in, email-verified account to read or write your data.
  • The operator, technically. The owner of a Firebase project can open everything stored in it from the Firebase console, and no software rule can prevent that. If you run your own project this is you. If someone else runs your copy, only sign up if you trust them, and ask them what they do with the data. Otherwise set up your own copy.
  • Google, as the host of Firebase Authentication and Cloud Firestore, acting for the operator. Servers may be outside your country.
  • Your AI provider, only for the requests described above.

The lazyfill code itself sends nothing to its authors, sells nothing, and contains no analytics, trackers or ads.

Keeping and deleting your data

  • Your data is kept until you delete it.
  • Delete everything: in the extension, open the dashboard, then Settings, then Delete my account. This deletes your cloud data and your account, and clears the extension’s data in your browser. Copies in Google’s backups are removed on Google’s schedule.
  • Take a copy first: the Applications page exports CSV and JSON, and Profiles exports JSON.
  • Removing the extension clears what it holds in your browser but does not delete your cloud account; use Delete my account first.
  • If you are the operator, deleting the Firebase project removes everything stored in it.

Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example the EU/UK under the GDPR, or California under the CCPA/CPRA) you may have the right to access, correct, delete, export or restrict the use of your data, and to complain to a regulator. Most of this you can do yourself in the app as above. For anything else, contact the operator of your copy: where such laws apply, the operator decides why and how the data is processed and is the one responsible for answering you. The lazyfill authors hold no data about you and cannot act on requests. Where the GDPR applies, the legal basis is providing the service you asked for (contract) and, for the optional AI features, your choice to use them (consent), which you can withdraw by not using them or removing your key.

Security

Data travels over HTTPS. Access is limited by Firebase Authentication and Firestore security rules. Email verification is required before any data is saved. No system is perfectly secure; keep your password private and your AI key secret.

Children

lazyfill is not directed at children and is not for anyone under 16.

For people who run a copy

If you let other people use your copy, you are the operator. This page is a template that describes the software as shipped; you are responsible for making sure it is accurate for your setup, for keeping the Firestore rules in place, and for answering requests from your users. Edit web/public/privacy.html and add your own name and contact details before you invite anyone. If you only run it for yourself, nothing more is needed.
